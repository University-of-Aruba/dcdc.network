Extending the DCDC Network to the Research Data Alliance: a conference perspective

From 5 to 9 October, I was in London, UK, to attend the Research Data Alliance (RDA) 27th Plenary, presenting a poster on the Dutch Caribbean Digital Competence Network (DCDC-Network)!

The DCDC-Network poster created for the Research Data Alliance Conference, providing a short summary of the Network’s onboarding survey results. The poster on Zenodo.

About the DCDC Network

The Dutch Caribbean Digital Competence (DCDC) Network was established in 2025 with funding from Open Science NL. It connects the Universities of Aruba, Curaçao, and St. Martin, and Instituto Pedagogico Arubano, with a shared mission: to make research data more reusable, accessible, and transparent across the Dutch Caribbean.

The current data landscape in our region is fragmented, which makes research collaboration and validation harder than it needs to be. The Network addresses this through peer-to-peer exchange, a training program, and strategic partnerships between institutions.

The Plenary had several co-located events, of which I attended one on the 5th of October, the Ethical Data Initiative Stakeholder Forum titled “Framing the Future of the Global Research Commons”, with two panels on “Why research commons and for whom? The governance of data sharing” and “The practice of data as commons: Reconciling Research Data Management Principles”. Particularly the second panel held interesting considerations of the practical application of the CARE principles. The CARE principles, described in the publication by Carroll et al. (2020), were developed to advance innovation, governance, and self-determination among Indigenous Peoples. Are we diluting the CARE principles if they are applied to communities that are not Indigenous? Is the application of the CARE principles even possible when the community under study is not a community with a clear boundary? My research study focusing on The Turing Way could follow the CARE principles (because I’m a member of the community and the study has been done in careful consultation with the community), but stating that it follows the CARE principles may still dilute the core values of CARE principles as The Turing Way is not an Indigenous community. More careful consideration of the practical application of the CARE principles, and an update of the recently added CARE principle page to The Turing Way, might be needed.

Esther posing in front of the DCDC-Network poster.

I met up with Tycho Hofstra from the GHOST collective, and was promised a copy of the Open Science Against Humanity game to bring back to Aruba! This game aims to raise awareness for Open Science and Open Science practices in a more playful way.

Nicole Emmenegger invited me to the assessment committee of the TDCC-SSH calls to provide a Dutch Caribbean perspective in the evaluation of the proposals aimed to address challenges in the Social Sciences and Humanities domains related to FAIR data (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable and Reusable).

I managed to score some cool CaSDaR stickers, and meet Samantha Pearman-Kanza and Louise Saul who are leading this initiative/network to empower Data Stewards with career paths and recognition. We exchanged some thoughts on career progression for Data Stewards and how unequal career progression may also relate to gender.

Collected stickers during the RDA, from RaID (persistent identifiers for research projects), CaSDaR (the cat stickers), TDCC-SSH, and a sticker about Metadata.

It was great to see interest into the DCDC-Network and input from the Caribbean region from the Professionalising Data Stewardship Interest Group! Liise Lehtsalu, one of the group’s chairs, was particularly keen to see how we can make existing groups in the RDA more accessible to the Caribbean Region. We (Curtis Sharma, Adira Daniel, Ayishih Bellew, Michelle Harricharan and myself) are moving towards this by organizing the RDA Birds of a Feather session in the near future: Stronger Together: Building a Caribbean Research Data Community. This session provides a pathway to join the RDA for Caribbean based data professionals.

During the plenary sessions the need for strong research communities was stressed (such as the DCDC-Network!). The need for equity in the research landscape was also repeatedly indicated, noting that capacity is not an equal playing field, raising the question: What does equity mean when people have access to different resources? A question we struggle with daily in the DCDC-Network.

Esther introducing the session of the Physical Samples Interest Group, together with Lesley Wyborn.

I co-led the session of the Interest Group sessions on Physical Samples, together with Lesley Wyborn. We discussed the latest updates regarding persistent identifiers and metadata for physical samples, and hosted a successful live demo of a lab that has implemented best practices around physical sample management successfully under the coordination of David Elbert.

The chairs (Esther Plomp, Lesley Wyborn) and speakers (David Elbert, Rossella Aversa and Simon Hodson) of the Physical Sample Interest Group Session.

During the Reproducibility Interest Group session I found Carole Goble’s point interesting: concepts such as Reproducibility may no longer be helpful to advance discussions. Carole pointed out that researchers are currently talking more about provenance instead of reproducibility, as verification/transparency is more important than reproducibility (generating the same results as the original study). Provenance is perhaps a concept that humanities scholars can also relate more to, as repeating or generalizing results is generally not the aim of this type of research. Meznah Aloqalaa shared her experiences around computational reproducibility during this session, which made me visit her poster, where we discussed how she managed to get ~300 responses from researchers on a survey she set out to ask about computational reproducibility challenges!

You can find summarizing blogposts of the RDA Plenary 27 on the RDA website (day 1, day 2, day 3), or sign up to join the RDA to stay informed! From my side I hope to welcome you for our upcoming online Birds of a Feather session on Stronger Together: Building a Caribbean Research Data Community, to learn more about how international networks such as the Research Data Alliance can be of help to data professionals based in the Caribbean.
